Quote a report, its scope and its CAGR — or quote nothing.

The A1 pitch circulated numbers that did not survive checking. This analysis replaces them. Scope is stated every time, because “reference-management software” and “scholarly publishing” differ by two orders of magnitude. Baethge & Jergas 2025, Research Integrity and Peer Review 10(1):13. The earlier pooled estimate (Jergas & Baethge 2015, PeerJ 3:e1364) gives 25.4% total / 11.9% major across 28 studies.

Nobody downstream is catching it

  • Reviewers missed two-thirds of major errors in a fictitious-manuscript experiment with 203 reviewers (Baxt et al., Ann Emerg Med 1998).
  • Reviewers found a mean of 3 of 9 planted errors across 607 BMJ reviewers. Training barely helped (Schroter et al., J R Soc Med 2008).
  • A BMJ 2023 analysis states responsibility for auditing cited literature “rests with those who selected it, not reviewers”, and proposes AI tools integrated into submission portals to confirm cited works exist and flag likely mis-citations (BMJ 2023;383:e076441).

That last item is ClaimTrace's value proposition, already published in a medical journal. It is the most useful citation this project has.

Retracted work keeps being cited

Across independent samples from 1990 to 2026, roughly 70–94% of retracted papers continue to be cited after retraction, most of those citations neutral or positive, and typically only 4–18% of citing articles mention the retraction.

  • 93.9% of 994 retracted Nature Index articles kept receiving citations — 21,047 post-retraction citations, 39.79% of them positive (J Inf Sci 2026).
  • The retracted Wakefield autism paper has been cited 1,154 times after retraction versus 643 before — it is cited more after retraction than before.
  • 52.5% of the 200 most recent citing articles inappropriately cited the retracted Surgisphere COVID papers (Science, Jan 2021).

The infrastructure to detect this is free and already public: on 12 September 2023 Crossref acquired the Retraction Watch database and released it CC0 — about 43,000 records, plus Crossref's own 14,000.

AI has made it acute, and dated

Fabricated citations rose more than 12-fold in three years, from 1 in 2,828 papers in 2023 to about 1 in 277 in the first seven weeks of 2026 — and 98.4% of affected papers saw no publisher action. That comes from auditing 2,471,758 PMC articles and 125.6M references (Lancet 2026).

Separately, GPT-4o produced wholly fabricated references in 19.9% of cases and erred in 45.4% of the genuine ones (JMIR Mental Health 2025, n = 176 — treat the exact rate as indicative). GPT-3.5 fabricated 55% and erred in 43% (Walters & Wilder, Sci Rep 2023, 636 citations).

03 — Competitive landscapeFour classes of incumbent, and one empty column

The incumbents

Reference managers — Zotero, Mendeley, EndNote. Store and format metadata. Zotero alone has 14.4M users in 2024, up 25% year on year. They do not read the cited paper.

Similarity checkers — Turnitin, iThenticate. Match text, and in their own words nothing more. Turnitin's help centre: “Turnitin does not check for plagiarism.” iThenticate's user guide: “its primary function is to highlight matching content from other sources.” A US Naval Postgraduate School guide lists what iThenticate does not identify, including “information or language attributed to the wrong source.”

Reference checkers — ReciteWorks, Edifix, CiteTrue, Paperpile, SciSpace. Confirm the record exists. Asked whether it checks that quoted text appears in the cited source, ReciteWorks' own FAQ answers “No, not currently.”

Citation-intent platforms — scite.ai, Semantic Scholar. Classify how other people cited a paper. Semantic Scholar's intent is purpose (background / method / result), not stance, and in a live API test only about 12% of citations carried any intent at all.

Function 2: Reference Check

scite's second relevant feature takes a manuscript PDF and reports, per reference, how it has been cited by others — retractions, editorial notices, contrasting findings. Pricing: $20/mo Basic, $50/mo Pro, both billed yearly; no institutional price published.

Where it stops. The unit of analysis is still the cited paper, and the evidence is still what third parties did with it. It tells you reference #12 has been retracted. It cannot tell you that your sentence about #12 is wrong — and to its credit, it does not claim to. There is also no published accuracy figure for Reference Check; Smart Citations has a peer-reviewed evaluation and this feature does not.

05 — PositionTwo layers, and a rule about failure

Audit — the record. Does the reference exist, and do title, authors, year and venue agree? OpenAlex first, Crossref as fallback, both keyless.

Verify — the meaning. Does the cited source actually support the sentence it is attached to? The supporting or contradicting passage is shown, not reduced to a label.

The failure rule — the genuinely unique part. A failed lookup stays LOOKUP_FAILED. NOT_FOUND means the search completed and found nothing; it is never converted into a claim that a reference was fabricated (audit-contract.md §2, §4).

No competitor documents this distinction. It matters because the most damaging output this class of tool can produce is not a wrong label — it is a reassuring one. A product that turns a timeout into “looks fabricated”, or an ingestion miss into a clean bill of health, is worse than no product.

06 — CorrectionsWhat changed from the A1 pitch, and why

Four figures in the A1 deck should not be repeated. Replacing them is the main analytical output of this exercise.

“12,402 papers, 7.1%”

A fused citation. 12,402 / 7.1% comes from an unrefereed vendor blog analysis (June 2026, 54 biomedical journals, severity subset only 1.4%); the adjacent “375 retracted / 76%” is a UFPE doctoral thesis on Latin American retractions 2002–2022. The “9,662” appears in neither.

Now: 16.9% of quotations incorrect, 8.0% major across 46 studies and 32,074 quotations — Baethge & Jergas 2025.

“Reference-management market $371M → $617M, 7.6% CAGR”

Traceable to a commercial vendor, but other vendors size the same market three times higher. Not defensible as a point estimate.

Now: vendors shown 3× apart ($371M–$1,150M), plus Clarivate's audited $1,266.0M SEC figure.

“GhostCite: 76.7% of reviewers…” presented as a survey

Not a survey. It is arXiv:2602.06718 (preprint) plus an IEEE S&P 2026 poster, and 76.7% is roughly 25 of 32 reviewers inside a 94-respondent AI/security subset.

Removed. The BMJ 2023 analysis and the reviewer-error experiments carry the point on better evidence.

“scite acquired for $20.9M, 2M+ users”

$20.9M is the initial consideration net of cash acquired; the earn-out took the total higher.

Now: ≈$29M all-in, with the $21.1M initial / $15.4M earn-out breakdown given if asked.

“Overleaf 25M+ / 30M users”

Overleaf's own about page says “20 million+ users” and also “over 30 million people trust Overleaf” — internally inconsistent.

Now: 20M+, quoting the smaller explicit figure and saying which one.

Retained unchanged: GPT-4o 19.9% / 45.4%, GPT-3.5 55% / 43%

These hold up and are used above.
